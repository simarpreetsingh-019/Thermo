How to check your MacBook's temperature, and cool it down.

By Simarpreet Singh · Updated 20 November 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: macOS doesn't show temperatures, so install a free sensor app. Thermo shows the exact CPU, GPU, battery and SSD temperature beside your notch and tells you which app is heating your Mac. Normal is about 35–60°C at light use and 70–95°C under heavy load. To cool down: quit the busiest app, use a hard surface, and move somewhere cooler.

Why macOS doesn't show the temperature

Activity Monitor shows CPU and memory use, but not degrees. macOS only tells apps a vague "thermal state" (nominal, fair, serious, critical). The real numbers come from dozens of hardware sensors inside your Mac, which a monitoring app can read without admin rights.

The quickest way: Thermo (free)

Thermo for Mac is a free, open-source app that sits around your MacBook's notch (or in the menu bar on any Mac). It runs on Apple Silicon (M1–M5) and Intel Macs with macOS 13 or later.

  • Exact temperature of every part: CPU performance and efficiency cores, GPU, memory, SSD, battery, Wi-Fi and palm rest, each against its own safe range.
  • Why it's hot: names the app causing the heat (even which project a node or python process belongs to), with one-click quit.
  • How long to cool: an estimate of when it'll be back to normal.
  • Alerts if it stays hot, plus 24-hour history and battery health.
  • About 16 MB of memory, no admin rights, no telemetry.

Can you check it in Terminal?

On Intel Macs, sudo powermetrics --samplers smc printed the CPU temperature. On Apple Silicon that sampler is gone, and powermetrics -s thermal only says the thermal pressure level, not degrees. Thermo adds a thermo command that needs no sudo: thermo temps, thermo status --json, or thermo headroom before a heavy build.

MacBook, Mac Studio, Mac mini, iMac, Mac Pro

Every Mac has a different set of sensors. Thermo reads what your model has: MacBooks show battery and palm rest, desktops don't. Mac Studio, Mac mini, iMac and Mac Pro get Thermo in the menu bar instead of the notch. Readings differ between apps because Apple publishes no official sensor map; Thermo shows each part on its own so you can see exactly what is hot.

What's a normal MacBook temperature?

Typical ranges for Apple Silicon (M1 to M5) MacBooks:

PartNormalWarmHot
CPU / GPU35–70°C70–90°C95°C+ (throttles)
Battery25–35°C35–40°C45°C+
SSD30–50°C50–60°C70°C+
Palm rest25–33°C33–38°C43°C+

Short spikes to 90°C+ while compiling, exporting or gaming are expected. What matters is staying hot for minutes: that's when macOS slows the chip down (throttling), and builds, renders and games get noticeably slower.

8 ways to cool a hot Mac down

  1. Quit the app causing it. One runaway app (a stuck browser tab, a build, a Docker container) is the most common cause. Thermo's Heat tab names it.
  2. Move to a cooler room. Yes, it works: a Mac can only cool to a little above room temperature. An air-conditioned room lowers both idle and peak temperatures, especially on fanless MacBook Airs.
  3. Use a hard, flat surface. Beds, sofas and laps block airflow. A laptop stand helps even more.
  4. Keep the vents clear and don't cover the keyboard area on Airs.
  5. Unplug the charger for a while if the battery is warm; charging adds heat.
  6. Close video and 3D: tabs playing video, games and 3D apps keep the GPU busy.
  7. Lower screen brightness and disconnect displays you're not using.
  8. Restart if nothing obvious is busy; a stuck background process may be the cause.

Thermo shows a cooldown estimate and tells you when it's back to normal, so you know whether to wait or close something.

Other Mac temperature apps

Good tools, depending on what you need. Pick Thermo for Mac if you want to know why your Mac is hot and what to close, or to measure your own code; pick a fan-control app if you want to set fan speeds yourself.

What Thermo does that the others don't: it names the app (and project) heating your Mac and how long cooling will take, lives around the notch, records benchmarks with a before/after table, and gives AI agents an MCP server and a JSON CLI. What it doesn't do: fan control. Use TG Pro or Macs Fan Control for that.

FAQ

Can a hot Mac get damaged?

Rarely. Apple chips protect themselves by slowing down, and shut down if needed. Heat mainly costs speed and, over years, battery health. Keeping the battery cool is the best long-term habit.

Why is my MacBook Air hotter than a Pro?

The Air has no fan, so it relies on slowing the chip to cool. A cooler room and a hard surface make a bigger difference on an Air.

Why do temperature apps show different numbers?

Apple Silicon has dozens of sensors and Apple publishes no official mapping, so each app picks its own sensors and calls the result "CPU". Some show the hottest core, others an average. Thermo shows each part separately (performance cores, efficiency cores, GPU, memory, SSD, battery) and labels which reading it uses as the hottest.

Why can't App Store apps show Mac temperature?

Sandboxed Mac App Store apps can't read the hardware sensors (SMC), so many store apps only show CPU usage or a thermal state. Temperature apps such as Thermo, Stats, TG Pro and iStat Menus are downloaded directly or installed with Homebrew.

Does Thermo show fan speed or control fans?

Not yet. Thermo focuses on temperatures, throttling and what is causing the heat, and needs no admin rights. For manual fan control use Macs Fan Control or TG Pro.

Can my AI coding assistant check the temperature?

With Thermo, yes. Its built-in MCP server lets Claude, Codex, Cursor and others check heat and memory before heavy jobs and wait for the Mac to cool. See the agent guide.
